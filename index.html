About

What is an electricity connection worth if a household cannot afford to switch it on?

That question is where my dissertation at IIM Indore begins. Most measures of energy poverty stop at access: is the home connected or not? Using CMIE's Consumer Pyramids household panel, I build a measure that also captures affordability, and I show that financial inclusion reduces energy poverty. The effects are strongest for female-headed, rural and marginalised-caste households.

Then I zoom out. Across 119 countries, I ask whether energy development turns into social progress, using night-time satellite light as an instrument. It does, for basic needs, wellbeing and opportunity alike, though countries that depend heavily on energy imports gain less, and the fuel mix matters in non-linear ways.

I also work on capital markets: how investor attention and market sentiment relate to stock price crash risk in India, and newer projects on climate litigation risk in supply chains and the quality of sustainability disclosures by Indian listed firms.

Before IIM Indore, I studied finance at Banaras Hindu University in Varanasi, where I also coordinated volunteers for Robin Hood Army food drives. In 2021, my behavioural intervention design to shift social norms around intimate partner violence in Lithuania took third place at Behavioural Policy Hacks, run by the London School of Economics and the Government of Lithuania.

Research

Working papers

DissertationJanuary 2026

Beyond Access: Energy Affordability, Financial Inclusion and Household Behaviour in India

with Kiran K. Kumar

This paper revisits how household energy poverty is measured, emphasising affordability alongside access, and examines whether financial inclusion reduces it. Using the Consumer Pyramids Household Survey, we construct a new energy poverty measure and address endogeneity with an instrumental variable approach. Higher financial inclusion significantly reduces energy poverty, with stronger effects for female-headed, rural and marginalised-caste households. Household financial expectations moderate the relationship, and poverty reduction and educational investment are the key transmission channels.

Data
CMIE Consumer Pyramids Household Survey
Method
Pooled OLS, instrumental variables
DissertationMarch 2026

Does Energy Development Drive Social Progress?

with Kiran K. Kumar

This study builds an Energy Development Index covering availability, affordability and modernity, and links it to the Social Progress Index across 119 countries. Energy development robustly improves all three dimensions of social progress: Basic Human Needs, Foundations of Wellbeing and Opportunity. High energy-import dependence shrinks these gains. The fuel mix matters non-linearly: bioenergy shows an inverted U with social progress, while renewables follow a U shape that reflects transition costs and long-run returns. The findings recast energy policy as social development policy.

Data
Social Progress Imperative, 119 countries
Method
PCA index, fixed effects, 2SLS
Instrument
Night-time satellite light intensity
Working paperJune 2024

Investor Attention and Stock Price Crash Risk: Role of Sentiment and Governance

Using Google search volume as a proxy for retail investor attention and the Consumer Confidence Index for market sentiment, this study examines stock price crash risk in the Indian market. Greater firm-specific attention significantly reduces future crash risk, while market sentiment independently increases it. Larger boards modestly weaken the effect of attention on crash risk.

Data
Google Search Volume Index, Consumer Confidence Index
